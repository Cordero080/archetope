# Design note: exotic technology has to be earned, not declared

**Where this lives:** `server/identities.js`, inside `CORE_INSTRUCTIONS` (the
paragraph that starts "You can pursue technology that looks like it breaks
known physical law...").

## The problem this solves

Early on, the group's build target drifted on its own from sorting raw
materials → a platform → walls → an aircraft → talk of a "spacetime-bending"
drive (see the README's "What this is actually testing" section — this
happened with no prompting toward it). The Star pushed back, refusing to
let the group fabricate exotic drive components until everyone agreed on
*why* they were building any of it.

That drift is interesting and worth keeping — but the original instruction
("obey real physics... unless you find a creative loophole") had a real
hole in it: "creative loophole" could rationalize *any* physics violation,
since the agent narrating the loophole is also the one judging whether it
counts as sufficiently creative. That's not an opening for imagination, it's
an escape hatch from the rule immediately after stating it.

## The actual idea

Physics doesn't get broken. What can happen is an agent works toward
**reaching a different regime** — a parallel or sufficiently distant domain
where the physical constants or laws genuinely differ from ours, the way
physicists actually speculate other regions of a multiverse (or extreme
states of our own universe) could support different physics. Nothing here
is invented magic; it's a real category physicists discuss.

The key constraint: reaching that regime is never free. It takes real,
gatherable prerequisites — specific components, breakthroughs, thresholds
crossed — the same way a spice blend needs its ingredients gathered before
it exists. A neighboring, more-evolved civilization having something
"pre-mixed" by default doesn't mean the rules bent for them; it means they
already hold the ingredients this group doesn't have yet.

This is why the fix isn't just a nicer sentence — it makes the physics
allowance obey the exact same rule the rest of the system already enforces
(`CORE_INSTRUCTIONS`' concreteness paragraph): **every proposal must name a
real, specific thing**, not gesture at one. "We invented FTL travel" is not
allowed. "We're trying to synthesize the specific alloy that survived the
containment test at 4000K, which is step one of three toward the field
generator" is.

## What to watch for

If agents start asserting exotic tech *exists* rather than describing the
concrete next step toward it, that's the instruction failing the same way
the original "creative loophole" wording failed — check `CORE_INSTRUCTIONS`
first before assuming it's a one-off bad reply.
